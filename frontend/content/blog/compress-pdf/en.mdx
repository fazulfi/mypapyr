# How to Compress a PDF: A Complete Guide

Large PDF files are slow to upload, hard to email, and awkward to share. Compressing a PDF reduces its file size while keeping the content readable. This guide explains what PDF compression actually does, when it helps, and how to compress a PDF online in seconds with the free [Compress PDF tool](/en/compress-pdf).

## What happens when you compress a PDF

A PDF can contain text, fonts, images, and vector shapes. Most of the size of a typical PDF comes from its images. Compression re-encodes those images with more efficient settings: lower resolution where detail is not needed, smarter color handling, and cleaner metadata. Text is kept sharp because it is stored as text, not as a picture.

## When compression helps

Compression is useful when you need to stay under a size limit, such as an email attachment limit or an upload limit on a website. It is also useful when you want the file to open faster on a slow connection. Compression is less useful for files that are already small, such as a simple text document with no images, because there is little to gain.

## What compression does not do

Compression does not remove pages, change the order of your document, or alter the meaning of the content. It does not convert your PDF into an image. And it cannot add detail that was never in the file: if a scanned page is blurry at its original size, compressing it will not make it sharper.
