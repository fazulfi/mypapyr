# How to Convert JPG to PDF: Images to a Single Document

Converting JPG images into a PDF is useful for receipts, photographed notes, scanned forms, and image sets that need one shareable file. A good conversion keeps the page order clear and fits each image predictably. This guide explains the process with Papyr's free [JPG to PDF tool](/en/jpg-to-pdf).

## Why convert images

A PDF is often easier to print, attach, or review than many separate image files. Combining images into one document also gives the recipient one download instead of a folder of pages. The conversion does not improve a blurry photograph, so capture quality and lighting still matter.

## Prepare your images

Review the images before uploading. Remove duplicates, rotate incorrectly captured pages, and decide the intended order. Use descriptive local filenames when sorting, then verify the order in the upload list. Very large camera images may create a larger PDF than necessary; resizing an original copy can help when the final document is only for on-screen reading.

## Check the result

Open the PDF and inspect every page. Look for sideways images, cropped edges, unreadable small text, or unexpected page order. If a photograph includes a dark border, the border remains part of the image unless you crop the source first. For forms, make sure all fields and signatures are visible at normal zoom.

## Common mistakes

Mixing images from two documents can make the final order confusing. Another mistake is judging readability from a thumbnail rather than the full page. Keep the original images until the PDF has been checked and stored. Do not upload images containing information you are not authorized to process.

**Tip:** photograph pages on a flat surface with even light and keep the camera parallel to the page for more consistent output.
